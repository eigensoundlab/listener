## Sources and what was assumed

- **Temporal integration, 1 kHz tone, moderate level.** dB the 5 ms tone must be raised to match the 200 ms tone. Florentine, Buus & Poulsen (1996), JASA 99(3):1633-1644; 6 listeners [established]. Assumed: both tones at 56 dB SPL before matching; 1 ms raised-cosine ramps.
- **Temporal integration, 1 kHz tone, high level.** dB the 5 ms tone must be raised. Florentine, Buus & Poulsen (1996): about 10 dB near 100 dB SPL [established]. Assumed: both tones at 100 dB SPL; 'about 10 dB' taken as 9 to 11.
- **Temporal integration, broadband noise, moderate level.** dB the 5 ms noise must be raised. Florentine, Buus & Poulsen (1996): 18-19 dB near 76 dB SPL for noise [established]. Assumed: noise 20 Hz to 20 kHz at 76 dB SPL; 1 ms ramps.
- **Temporal integration, broadband noise, high level.** dB the 5 ms noise must be raised. Florentine, Buus & Poulsen (1996): about 13 dB near 100 dB SPL for noise [established]. Assumed: noise at 100 dB SPL; 'about 13 dB' taken as 12 to 14.
- **A rising tone is louder than the same tone falling.** dB the falling tone must be raised to match the rising one. Ponsot, Susini & Meunier (2015), Atten. Percept. Psychophys. 77(3):907-920; 10 to 16 listeners, two methods [established]. Assumed: 1 kHz, 2 s, 60 to 75 dB SPL linear in dB (the paper used 15 dB ramps within 45-90 dB SPL).
- **An impact-like envelope is softer than its time reverse (tone).** dB the reversed (slow attack, fast decay) sound must be lowered to match the impact-like one. Stecker & Hafter (2000), JASA 107(6):3358-3368; 250 ms envelopes of equal energy; about 1-3 dB for one listener [single study; the sign is the finding, the size is weak]. Assumed: 2 ms attack, exponential decay of 40 dB over 250 ms; the paper's time constants were not available.
- **An impact-like envelope is softer than its time reverse (noise).** dB the reversed (slow attack, fast decay) sound must be lowered to match the impact-like one. Stecker & Hafter (2000), JASA 107(6):3358-3368; 250 ms envelopes of equal energy; about 1-3 dB for one listener [single study; the sign is the finding, the size is weak]. Assumed: 2 ms attack, exponential decay of 40 dB over 250 ms; the paper's time constants were not available.
- **A slow train of impulses is as loud as one impulse.** dB the train must be raised to match the single impulse (none). Ogura, Suzuki & Sone (1991), J. Acoust. Soc. Jpn (E) 12(1):1-11: no change up to 0.2-0.7 impulses a second [single study]. Assumed: noise impulse of 50 ms, three of them 4 s apart (0.25 a second); 'as loud' taken as within 1 dB.
- **A fast train of impulses is louder than one impulse.** dB the train must be lowered to match the single impulse. Ogura, Suzuki & Sone (1991): above the critical rate loudness grows with rate [single study; sign only]. Assumed: the same impulse, ten of them 0.2 s apart (5 a second).
- **A 63 Hz octave band of noise against a 1 kHz band.** dB the 63 Hz band must be raised to match the 1 kHz band. Fenton & Lee (2017), AES 143rd Convention: listeners gave 9.98 dB where BS.1770 gives 2.73; 13 listeners, loudspeakers [single study]. Assumed: octave bands of pink noise, both at 75 dB SPL; 9.98 dB taken as 9 to 11.
- **A louder beginning counts for more than a louder middle.** dB the sound with the louder middle must be raised to match the one with the louder beginning. Fischenich, Hots, Verhey, Guldan & Oberfeld (2021), PLoS ONE 16(12):e0261001: onset weight 4-5 times the later weight, time constant 200-300 ms [established; sign only]. Assumed: 1 s noise at 65 dB SPL, 6 dB more in the first 100 ms or from 500 to 600 ms.
- **The same impact dry and with a 2 s reverberant tail.** dB the wet version would be raised (negative: lowered) to match the dry one. no listener data found (perception_and_measures.md, section 3) [open question]. Assumed: noise impact of 0.3 s; tail 12 dB under its peak, decaying 60 dB in 2 s.
- **A 30-150 Hz boom against a 2-6 kHz click, around 60 dB SPL.** dB the boom would be raised to match the click. no listener data found (perception_and_measures.md, section 4) [open question]. Assumed: click 30 ms; boom 1.2 s with a 250 ms decay; both built from noise of the stated level.
- **A 30-150 Hz boom against a 2-6 kHz click, around 80 dB SPL.** dB the boom would be raised to match the click. no listener data found (perception_and_measures.md, section 4) [open question]. Assumed: click 30 ms; boom 1.2 s with a 250 ms decay; both built from noise of the stated level.
